## Durability and custody

Callbacks enter a bounded queue. Queue acceptance is not durability. A dedicated
writer uses SQLite WAL with FULL commits. The durable sequence advances only
after commit. A queue overflow, conversion failure, quota, reserve, or write error
latches capture failure. The plugin logs the transition once and stops callback
work. The failure stays visible. It does not claim continuous capture after loss.

Native event JSON is compressed in the journal, with a digest of the original
bytes and bounded decompression. Root/bank IDs, transaction and entry indexes,
entry ranges, metadata counts, parent chain, and final entry hash are checked.
Initial partial roots are listed as startup gaps. Later incomplete roots fail.

The worker retains full native source evidence, compressed with zstd, beside the
compact archive. A receipt binds both original source bytes and stored object
bytes. The worker checks the CLI commit before it saves its cursor. A durable
pending-batch record keeps the exact range across a crash. The CLI preserves an
interrupted stage and rebuilds from durable normalization.

Only after compact custody and cursor commit does the worker request retirement.
The capture writer checks that proof and deletes exact committed rooted bank
records in bounded FULL transactions. It acknowledges only after durable delete.
SQLite free pages are reused. Other forks, bankless records, late records, startup
records, and unrooted tail records remain. A durable audit checks late callbacks
against per-bank commits; it reads retained source if journal rows were retired.

## Limits that must remain visible

- Compact and retained-source storage is finite. R2 upload is not configured.
  Other-fork and startup records plus receipts also grow. Archive output needs a
  storage policy; source retirement alone does not give unlimited retention.
- A plugin load starts partial coverage. A clean unload leaves an unrooted tail.
  Old unclean sessions are listed but are not silently repaired.
- Retirement runs in the live writer. Requests created after a clean unload stay
  pending with their source preserved. An offline drain tool is not implemented.
- The original shred recorder hit its quota and its producer latched an I/O
  failure. The parent found that a controlled validator restart is needed to
  restore it. Geyser compact output does not claim current or complete shreds.
- The old lost epoch interval is not recoverable from this validator's pruned
  local ledger. These tests do not repair it or prove a full epoch capture.
- Finality trusts the operator's rooted callback. `canonical=false` and
  `publication_ready=false`. PoH structure is checked, but cryptographic PoH and
  signature verification and full archive read-back are not run.
- Epoch geometry is mainnet-only and bound to the configured mainnet genesis.
